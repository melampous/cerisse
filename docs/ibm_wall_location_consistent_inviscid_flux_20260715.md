# Wall-location-consistent stationary no-slip IBM inviscid extension

Date: 2026-07-15

## Result

The opt-in LLF-WENO-Z5 and LLF-TENO5 IBM path now satisfies two separate
verification contracts for a smooth, fixed, isothermal no-slip wall:

1. the IBM crossing face and the first two same-fluid Cartesian faces have an
   inviscid numerical-flux defect of at least `O(h^3)`; and
2. the complete Navier--Stokes semi-discrete residual in the first off-wall
   fluid-cell layer converges at least as `O(h^2)`.

The first statement is a local flux-consistency result. It is not a claim that
the global solution is third order. The second statement is the requirement
needed for a globally second-order IBM discretization in smooth flow.

The currently certified scope is:

- two-dimensional Cartesian flow;
- one calorically perfect ideal-gas species and five conservative equations;
- stationary, constant-isothermal, no-slip wall;
- smooth analytic circular geometry for the formal refinement study;
- `ghost_layers=1`, visibility-filtered supports, and quadratic volume IBM;
- LLF-WENO-Z5 or LLF-TENO5 convection with the direct wall-consistent heat and
  viscous flux closure.

The feature remains opt-in. Unsupported combinations are rejected at compile
time or abort at runtime instead of silently using an unverified extension.

## Why the flux gate is third order

For the first fluid cell next to an immersed wall, the finite-volume inviscid
residual contains

\[
  R_i^I=-\frac{F^I_{i+1/2}-F^I_{i-1/2}}{h}.
\]

If either face has an independent `O(h^2)` flux defect, division by `h` can
leave an `O(h)` residual. The crossing face and the neighbouring same-fluid
faces must therefore share an `O(h^3)` flux contract. This is the inviscid
counterpart of the direct heat/stress closure's face-gradient requirement.

## Stationary no-slip extension

For each Cartesian solid/fluid crossing, the geometry audit stores one actual
first hit, its owning surface element, an oriented wall normal, and visible
real-fluid supports. Runtime primitive values are reconstructed from the
current Runge--Kutta stage; no directional ghost state is written to the
shared primitive `MultiFab`.

Let `s=0` be the first hit, with `s>0` directed from the wall into the fluid.
The adjacent solid-cell centre is at `s=-d_g`. Visible-fluid WLS traces are
evaluated on the same Cartesian line at `d_g` and `2d_g`. For a scalar with a
Dirichlet wall value, the quadratic extension is

\[
  q(-d_g)=q(2d_g)-3q(d_g)+3q_w.
\]

It is applied to all velocity components with `u_w=0` and to temperature with
the prescribed isothermal value. This enforces no slip at the actual wall
location rather than at a grid midpoint.

Pressure uses the stationary no-slip normal-momentum compatibility condition

\[
  \frac{\partial p}{\partial n}
  =\mathbf n\mathbin{\cdot}(\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol{\tau}).
\]

The sign follows directly from the wall-limit Navier--Stokes momentum
equation. A material point on a stationary no-slip wall has zero velocity and
zero material acceleration, so `grad(p)=div(tau)` there. Wall velocity
gradients and Hessians are supplied by a hard-Dirichlet constrained cubic WLS
fit. For

\[
 \tau_{ij}=\mu(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})
 +(\xi-2\mu/3)\theta\delta_{ij},\qquad
 \theta=u_{k,k},
\]

the implemented Cartesian divergence is

\[
 (\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}\tau)_i=
 \mu\nabla^2u_i
 +\mu_{,j}(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})
 +(\xi+\mu/3)\theta_{,i}
 +(\xi_{,i}-2\mu_{,i}/3)\theta.
\]

Pressure traces on the physical wall-normal line at `d_n` and `2d_n` give

\[
 p_w=\frac{4p(d_n)-p(2d_n)-2d_n p_n}{3}.
\]

The Cartesian-line pressure ghost then uses the same quadratic extension as
the other primitive variables. Density is recovered from `p_g,T_g` through
the equation of state, and total energy is recomputed from the resulting
thermodynamic state and velocity. Density and total energy are never
independently extrapolated.

## WLS support and fallback

The direct wall closure has a support block independent of the legacy volume
ghost-point store. In two dimensions it uses a visibility-filtered `4 x 4`
real-fluid block. The previous `3 x 3` block had only one redundant sample for
the eight-coefficient constrained cubic and produced phase-dependent Hessian
amplification. With the wider block, every crossing in the final
`64/96/128/192` sequence retained the cubic path.

Initialization checks support visibility, numerical rank, normal-matrix
condition number, finite weights, and scaled L1 weight amplification. The
explicit metadata ladder remains cubic, quadratic, normal-linear, then
invalid. The stationary no-slip inviscid overwrite requires the cubic level;
otherwise it leaves the conservative legacy fallback generated by the dense
pass. Strict verification mode aborts on any such degradation.

## Crossing and same-fluid flux assembly

At a crossing face, one face-local ghost state plus four real-fluid cells form
a contiguous translated five-cell LLF stencil. The selected WENO-Z5 or TENO5
operator performs characteristic reconstruction once and writes one
conservative flux into the ordinary face `FArrayBox`.

For each of the first two same-fluid faces whose nominal stencil intersects
the body, the code selects complete, contiguous, five-cell blocks from the
fluid side. Positive and negative LLF traces may use different translations,
but each separately preserves the full fifth-order moment conditions. No
solid primitive is admitted by deleting candidates from an incomplete
five-cell stencil.

The crossing and same-fluid fluxes therefore use the same LLF splitting and
the same shifted reconstruction family. Their only difference is the
physically constrained, first-hit-consistent state needed on the solid side.
The resulting flux remains in the normal finite-volume divergence, AMR
average-down, and reflux infrastructure.

## Audit findings and limits

The implementation meets the requested two-dimensional smooth-wall gate, but
the following claims are not yet justified:

- A three-dimensional CPU template compiles, but no 3-D sphere refinement
  sequence has established the flux or first-layer residual order. The 3-D
  direct support remains `3 x 3 x 3` and needs an independent conditioning
  audit.
- No fixed-time coupled sequence has yet been run with this new inviscid
  extension. The direct heat/stress subsystem already has fixed-time evidence,
  but the combined inviscid extension still needs that long-form regression.
- Strong shocks, sharp STL edges, narrow gaps, moving walls, adiabatic walls,
  RZ coordinates, multiple species, and variable-caloric thermodynamics are
  outside the certified path.
- The crossing characteristic transform currently uses the adjacent-cell Roe
  basis for compatibility with the legacy LLF path. Smooth convergence shows
  no order loss, but a face-local Roe basis should be assessed before strong
  shock production use.
- Shifted TENO5 recovers its exact signed optimal weights when all candidates
  are smooth. Candidate dropout near a discontinuity uses an engineering
  renormalization and is not, by itself, an entropy or positivity proof.
- The geometry audit is initialization-only, but the CUDA compiler reports a
  large stack frame and spills for its combined reconstruction kernel. This
  does not affect per-stage RHS cost, though splitting the initialization
  kernel is a worthwhile performance task for large 3-D STL cases.

Consequently, this change closes the smooth two-dimensional stationary
no-slip convective/pressure shell defect. Production certification still
requires a fixed-time full-NS sequence, a 3-D analytic sphere sequence, and a
shock/no-slip robustness matrix with admissibility monitoring.
