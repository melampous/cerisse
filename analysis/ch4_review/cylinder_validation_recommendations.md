## 2. Primary recommendation

Anchor the cylinder validation subsection on **two complementary comparisons**: (A) the Bashkin M = 1.7 surface benchmark for the viscous no-slip wall, and (B) a shock stand-off composite for the shock-capture + fore-body IBM (both wall models).

### Anchor A — Bashkin et al. (2002), M = 1.7 surface quantities (no-slip wall)

This is the de facto compressible-IBM cylinder benchmark: De Palma et al. (2006), de Tullio et al. (2007), Al-Marouf & Samtaney (2017), Yuan & Zhong (arXiv:1611.01447), and the Mittal group (M = 1.7, Re = 2×10⁵ computations in their 2025 review) all validate against exactly this configuration. Matching it puts CERISSE directly on the community yardstick.

**Figures/tables to produce:**
1. **Cp(θ) at M = 1.7** — CERISSE no-slip vs Bashkin experiment (digitized) and Bashkin q–ω URANS, with published IBM curves (De Palma, de Tullio, Yuan–Zhong; Section 3) as secondary lines.
2. **Table: Cd and θ_sep** — CERISSE values appended to the Yuan–Zhong Table 1 compilation (experiment Cd ≈ 1.43, θ_sep ≈ 112°; IBM literature Cd = 1.38–1.45, θ_sep = 113–119°).
3. **Cd(M) context figure** — Gowen & Perkins Cd(M) curve digitized from NACA TN 2960 with the Bashkin point and the CERISSE M = 1.7 point overlaid; their verified no-Re-effect finding is the explicit bridge from Re = 1e5 to the reference Re.

**Action item before running:** resolve the **Re-definition trap**. Bashkin's abstract states Re = 2×10⁵; Yuan & Zhong restate the identical case as Re_D = 4×10⁵ — consistent with Bashkin's value being radius-based. Sight the original paper, then either (i) run one dedicated leg at the benchmark Re, or (ii) keep Re = 1e5 and lean on the Gowen–Perkins Re-insensitivity finding, stated explicitly.

**Honest caveats to write down:** experiment is a 3-D wind-tunnel model (aspect-ratio/end effects, TsAGI tunnel interference undocumented by modern standards); the experimental Cd is pressure drag taken as total drag (Yuan–Zhong footnote, verified); the companion numerics are 2-D URANS (q–ω), not DNS/ILES, so URANS-vs-ILES differences are expected on the leeward side; θ_sep is a sensitive quantity — Yuan & Zhong themselves show a documented ~6° non-convergence vs experiment (consistent with the campaign's primary-vs-sensitive-quantity philosophy: lead with Cd, Cp, stand-off; report θ_sep with error bars, do not let it drive the verdict).

### Anchor B — Shock stand-off composite, δ/D(M) (Euler-slip and no-slip)

**Figure to produce:** δ/D vs M with (i) Sinclair–Cui eq. (32) as the theory line, (ii) their Euler CFD points, (iii) Alperin (1950) and Kim (1956) experimental points digitized from Sinclair–Cui Fig. 7 (open access), (iv) the Billig correlation curve (Δ/D = 0.97 at M = 1.7) as a dashed cross-check, and (v) CERISSE Euler-slip and no-slip points at M = 1.7. Expected band at M = 1.7: δ/D ≈ 1.0–1.1. A companion panel of δ vs AMR level against the closed-form correlation makes a clean grid-convergence exhibit.

**Caveats:** Kim's facility is a shock tube (finite test time, Re unverified — fore-body/stand-off quantities are defensible, wake quantities are not); Kim has no point at M = 1.7 (nearest 1.35/1.8), so the local bracket rests on Alperin; Billig is extrapolated below its M ≳ 2 calibration and sits ~5–10% low at M = 1.7 — present it as a correlation cross-check, not truth data.

### Strouhal number: do not promise a validation

Verified literature gap: no cylinder shedding measurements exist at M ≈ 1.3–2.5 (nothing surfaced to contradict this in verification). Frame any M = 1.7 wake unsteadiness via (i) the Murthy–Rose verbatim finding that periodic shedding ceases above M = 0.9, and (ii) the shear-layer/slipline-length-scaled Strouhal universality St_L ≈ 0.45–0.49 (Schmidt & Shepherd, *JFM* 785, R3, 2015, M = 4: St_D 0.30→0.50 over Re 2×10⁴–5×10⁵, slipline-based St ≈ 0.48; Thasu & Duvvuri, *Phys. Rev. Fluids* 7, L081401, 2022, M = 6: St_S = 0.49; Thasu, Kumar & Duvvuri, *JFM* 1010, A6, 2025: model limit ≈ 0.45) — with the explicit caveat that universality is demonstrated only for M ≥ 4 and its extension to M = 1.7 is untested (Gai, Talluru & Nandhan, arXiv:2408.02933, argue the geometry-only scaling is too restrictive). Raw St_D comparison against the M = 3 UNSW data (Awasthi et al., *JFM* 945, A4, 2022, DOI 10.1017/jfm.2022.517; *JFM* 1012, A27, 2025, DOI 10.1017/jfm.2025.10190, open CC-BY: Re_D = 6×10⁵, hydrodynamic St_d ≈ 0.2 + dominant aeroacoustic St_d ≈ 0.42) is **not defensible** at M = 1.7/Re = 1e5; cite for mechanism only.

## 4. Rejected or unverified candidates — do not cite without further action

**Rejected for this campaign:**
- **Mizukaki T., *J. Visualization* 11(2), 133–141 (2008)** — source exists, but geometry is unresolved and physical reasoning (5.56 mm rifle-caliber free-flight projectile) strongly indicates an *axially flying flat-faced axisymmetric body*, not a 2-D crossflow cylinder. Do not use unless the full text proves otherwise.
- **Dewey C.F. Jr., *Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer* 8, 245–252 (1965)** — real paper, but verified to be a low-Reynolds/rarefied (hot-wire regime, Re₀ = 2×10⁻²–10³, Knudsen-dependent) correlation. **Not applicable at Re_D = 1×10⁵**; the Angle-1 framing overstated its reach. (A 1977 follow-on at higher Re exists but still falls short.)
- **Motallebi & Norbury, *JFM* 110, 273–292 (1981)** — exists, but is a *blunt-trailing-edge body with base bleed*, not a circular cylinder. Never present as cylinder data.
- **Bashkin et al. 2000 companion (*Fluid Dynamics* 35, DOI 10.1023/A:1026630613133)** — real, but its Mach numbers are 2 and 5 (isothermal wall), **not 1.7**; cite only for turbulence-modeling context.

**Unverified — sight before any citation:**

**Corrections that must be carried into any thesis text (already applied above):**
- Bashkin 2002 computations are **q–ω URANS**, not laminar.
- Kim 1956 is a **shock-tube** experiment (not wind tunnel), pp. 439–445, with no test point at M = 1.7.
- Billig at M = 1.7 gives **Δ/R = 1.94 → Δ/D = 0.97** (the Angle-1 "Δ/R ≈ 1.0–1.1" figure was a radius/diameter mix-up); coefficient is 4.67, not 4.76; and Sinclair–Cui do **not** cite Billig, so do not chain those citations.
- Yuan & Zhong's "Ignatova and Karimullin" experiment label is wrong; the experiment is Bashkin et al. (2002).
- Canuto & Taira Re = 40 wake length at M = 0.5 is **2.53** (not 2.54); Inoue & Hatakeyama's Mach range is **0.05–0.3** (not 0.4) and force values are in text/figures, not a per-M table — relevant only if the secondary low-Mach legs (Re = 40/100/150 and the Williamson St–Re sweep, all bibliographically verified) are added as incompressible-regime regression cases.
